# LinkedIn post

> **Starter format.** Usable as it stands. Change it to how the team posts about a launch, or delete it and its row in `README.md`. The brackets under "Fill in" are the format: they stay. This box goes once the team has made the format its own.

Posts on LinkedIn, or another social channel, for a launch: one per person, each in that person's voice. Optional for Tier 2, always for Tier 3. A single post outside a launch uses the company's social post format in [`company/templates/`](../../../../company/templates/README.md).

## How to write this

- **Load first:** each author's `people/<name>/tone-of-voice.md` and their recent posts: how they open, how long their paragraphs are, whether they ask questions. Their voice comes before the company's. Then the agreed messaging brief.
- **This is not an announcement.** It is a post about building in public that happens to mention something new. The best ones don't feel like launches: they feel like someone sharing what they noticed, built or think about a problem.
- **Pick who makes sense.** Not everyone posts for every launch: two or three people is usually right. Choose by who is closest to the feature and who has a natural angle.
- **A different angle for each.** If you swap the names and the posts still work, they are too similar. The angles: the build (what was made, and why), the problem (a real situation), the customer story (a real interaction), the opinion (a stance), the reflection (what was learned).
- **What kills it:** "excited to announce", a list of features dressed up as insight, the corporate third person, engagement bait ("thoughts?"), humblebrags, and anything that sounds machine-written.
- **Length:** 100 to 200 words. Short and sharp beats long and thorough.
- **The final test:** would this person actually post it? If they would rewrite more than a fifth of it, the voice is wrong.
