# Drafts

What the designers are exploring, one folder each. The work itself happens in a design file or on a branch of the repo; the folder here says who is exploring it, what it is, where the work is and what it taught. Everything here is a proposal. The whole team can read it, and agents search it before starting similar work.

Status is one of: exploring, in review, shipped, dropped.

## Start a draft

1. **Copy [`_template/`](_template/README.md)** to `drafts/<subject>/`, for example `drafts/settings-navigation/`. The subject is the thing a teammate would recognise, not the round of work.
2. **Fill in its `README.md`:** who owns it, what is being explored and why, its status, and where the work is. "What I learned" fills in as you go.
3. **Add a row** to the table above.

## Keep it usable

- **A draft belongs to the person who started it.** Edit it for them, or with their yes.
- **One file page or one branch per draft.** Improve it in place. No `-v2` copies.
- **Start from what ships.** A draft builds on the real components, in the library or in the code, never on a copy drawn from scratch.
- **When a draft settles something, write it down.** A settled call becomes a rule in [`rules/`](../rules/README.md), a correction goes into this space's [`lessons.md`](../lessons.md). The draft's "What I learned" points to them.
- **When a draft grows into shared work,** a second person joins or it gets a deadline of its own, propose a project for it in [`projects/`](../../../projects/README.md) and leave a line here pointing to it.
- **Shipped and dropped drafts stay** with their status and what was learned, so nobody explores the same thing twice. Delete one only when it taught nothing.
