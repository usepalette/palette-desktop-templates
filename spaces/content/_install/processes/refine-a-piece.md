# Refine a piece

Tightens a draft against the playbook: one takeaway, clear early, concrete examples, and shorter than it was. It runs on a first draft, or whenever someone asks for a draft to be made better.

## When to run this

- Someone asks "tighten this draft", "make this better", "edit this using our playbook" or "this is too long".
- A first draft exists and the person wants it ready for someone else to read.

## Steps

1. **Read the piece and the playbook.** The piece, with its channel, its takeaway and who it goes out as. Then [`playbooks/content.md`](../playbooks/content.md): the principles, **While writing**, and the line for its channel in **Content types**. A text pasted into the conversation is refined there; offer once to save it as a piece.
2. **Check the spine first.** Say the takeaway in one sentence. If the piece says five things vaguely, say so and propose the one to keep before polishing anything. A shorter piece that says one thing well beats a longer one that says five things vaguely.
3. **Lead with the insight or the problem.** Move it to the top, so the takeaway is clear in the first three sentences. The backstory comes later, or goes.
4. **Make it concrete.** Replace an abstract claim with an example, an outcome or a situation the reader recognises, taken from `company/`, the projects or the piece's sources. Strong verbs over piles of adjectives; workflows and outcomes over lists of features.
5. **Cut.** Aim to remove about a fifth on the first pass. Cut, don't pad. Check the length against the channel's line in the playbook.
6. **Check every number, quote and name.** Each one has a source under **Notes**. One without a source is cut, or kept and marked "(to confirm)" for the person to check. Never invent one to make a line land.
7. **Rewrite in place.** One file per piece: improve it where it is, with no `-v2` copy. Update `last_updated` and `last_updated_by`. The status stays `draft` unless the author says the piece is ready for someone else to read.
8. **Show what changed.** The new version, then a short list of what was cut or moved, and why. Then offer the voice check.

## Done when

- The takeaway can be said in one sentence and is clear in the first three.
- The length fits the channel's line in the playbook, or the playbook has no length for that channel yet.
- Every number, quote and name has a source or a "(to confirm)" mark.
- The person has seen the new version.
