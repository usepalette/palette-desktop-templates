## How we think about it

Understand the customer's world before talking about ours. Discovery first, demo second. If we can't describe their problem better than they can, we haven't earned the right to pitch.

- The best sales conversations feel like consulting, not pitching.
- Customers buy when they feel understood, not when they feel impressed.
- Specifics beat superlatives. A proof point from [`company/brand/messaging.md`](../../../company/brand/messaging.md) → Proof points beats "revolutionary".
- If we are not right for them, say so early. Trust compounds.

## Before the call

- Read the account's brief and its last two meeting notes.
- Check their website, their LinkedIn and recent news.
- Have a hypothesis about their problem, and hold it loosely.
- Prepare discovery questions, not a slide deck.

## Discovery questions

Choose them for the account's stage and its open questions. Don't read them like a script.

- [Three questions about the problem our product solves, one per line]
- How do you handle [the problem we solve] today?
- What happens if this stays the way it is?
- Who else is involved in a decision like this?

## During the call

- **Discovery, most of the call (60 to 70%):** start with their world. Ask open questions about how they work today. Listen for pain, and write it down in their words.
- **Positioning (20 to 30%):** connect their pain to what we do, in their language. Show the workflow that addresses it. Be honest about what is live and what is coming.
- **Next steps (5 to 10%):** end with a specific next action, never "let's stay in touch".

## After the call

- Log it: say "here are my notes from the call". The note goes in the customer's meeting notes, and the brief is updated.
- Put the follow-ups where [`company/meetings.md`](../../../company/meetings.md) says follow-ups go, and link them from the brief's open questions.
- Send a follow-up within 24 hours.

## Qualification

**Good signals:**

- They describe a real pain, not a theoretical interest.
- They have tried to solve it before, which shows urgency.
- The person on the call can influence the decision.
- They ask detailed questions about how it works.
- [A signal particular to what we sell]

**Red flags:**

- "Just exploring", with no specific pain.
- No budget authority, and no path to it.
- They want a feature we don't have and won't build.
- The timeline is "someday".
- [A red flag particular to what we sell]

## What we don't do

- Pitch cold without research.
- Lead with a demo before we understand the problem.
- Promise timelines we can't keep.
- Discount to close. If price is the only objection, it is not the right fit.
- Use pressure: "limited time offer", "others are signing up".
