# Content

> **Not confirmed yet.** A starter playbook. Change it until it says how the team writes, then delete this box.

How we think about content: useful first, promotional second, and written from real work. Load it before writing anything the company will publish.

## How we think about it

Content is useful first and promotional second. If someone reads a piece and learns something, even if they never buy, it has done its job. The best content comes from real work: problems we solved, decisions we made, things we learned.

## Principles

- **Write from experience, not from research alone.**
- **One strong idea beats five mediocre ones.** Every piece has one clear takeaway.
- **Distribution matters as much as creation.** A great post nobody sees is a wasted post.
- **Consistency beats intensity.** Regular output beats sporadic bursts.

## Approach

### Before writing

- Read how the company sounds in [`company/brand/voice.md`](../../../company/brand/voice.md), and the author's `people/<name>/tone-of-voice.md` when it goes out under their name
- Check whether it has been covered before: the pieces in the Content space when the workspace has one, the projects in [`projects/`](../../../projects/README.md), and this space's [`research/`](../research/README.md)
- Define the one thing the reader should take away
- Know where it will be published, and shape it for that channel

### While writing

- Lead with the insight or the problem, not the backstory: the problem first, then the relief
- Use concrete examples: real situations, roles and tools. Specific beats abstract
- Talk in workflows and outcomes, not a list of features
- Keep paragraphs short, one idea each. Few adjectives, strong verbs
- Read it out loud. If you stumble, rewrite
- Cut 20% after the first draft. Then cut 10% more

### Before it goes out

Run the self-check:

1. Would I say this to a smart friend over coffee?
2. Is there a specific outcome or example?
3. Could a competitor say the same thing? If yes, it is too generic.
4. Am I leading with the problem or the product?
5. Would a sceptic roll their eyes? If yes, dial it back.

Then check that the takeaway is clear in the first three sentences. For a fuller check against the voice, the messaging and the strategy, run [`processes/review-gtm-work.md`](../processes/review-gtm-work.md).

## Content types

| Type | Length | Where | How often |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn post | 100 to 200 words | LinkedIn | two or three a week |
| Blog post | 500 to 1,500 words | The blog | once or twice a month |
| Case study | 800 to 1,200 words | The website | when one is earned |
| Newsletter | 300 to 500 words | Email | weekly or fortnightly |

## Distribution

Publishing is half the job. For every piece:

1. **The main channel.** Where it lives: the blog, LinkedIn, the newsletter.
2. **Amplification.** Share it in the communities, groups and threads where its readers are.
3. **Reuse.** A blog post can become three LinkedIn posts; a case study can become a talk.

## What we don't do

- Generic "thought leadership" with no original insight
- AI-written content published without heavy editing and a real person's perspective
- Chasing search keywords without something genuine to say
- Writing about topics we have no direct experience of

## Where a piece is written

In the Content space when the workspace has one (`spaces/content/`); otherwise in the project it belongs to, or in the person's scratchpad until it has a home. A launch piece starts from the formats in [`templates/launch/`](../templates/launch/README.md).
