# Plan distribution

Plans how a piece reaches people: where it lives, where it is shared and what else it becomes. The plan is given in the conversation, and saved only when someone asks.

## When to run this

- Someone asks "where should this go?", "plan how we distribute this" or "what else can this become?".
- A piece is about to go out, or has just gone out, with no plan.

## Steps

1. **Read the piece and the playbook.** The piece's channel and takeaway, then [`playbooks/content.md`](../playbooks/content.md) → Content types and Distribution: the channels the team uses, how often, and where it shares.
2. **Name the primary channel.** Where the piece lives: the blog, a LinkedIn profile, the newsletter. Usually its `channel`.
3. **Plan the amplification.** Where it is shared after that: the communities, groups, threads and partners under **Where we share** in the playbook, or ones the person names. A place you suggest that neither names is marked "(to confirm)". Never invent a community or a partner.
4. **Plan the repurposing.** What else the piece becomes, each with its own angle in one line: a blog post into three LinkedIn posts, each from a different angle, or a case study into a talk. Be specific about each spin-off.
5. **Set the order.** What goes out first and what follows, against the team's cadence in the playbook, so the channels don't all go on the same day.
6. **Give the plan in the conversation.** The primary channel, the amplification, the repurposing and the order, in a few lines. Save it only when the person asks: then it goes under **Notes** → Distribution in the piece itself, never in a file of its own.
7. **Spin-offs become pieces.** A spin-off the person wants written starts as its own piece, with [`start-a-piece.md`](start-a-piece.md), and its notes link back to the original.

## Done when

- The plan names the primary channel, where the piece is shared and what else it becomes, each on a channel the team uses or marked "(to confirm)".
- It is in the conversation, or in the piece's notes when the person asked to save it.
